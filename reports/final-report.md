## 1. Problem and motivation

Scholarly collaboration questions need three things that no single source provides: complete publication records, a reliable notion of "the same researcher" across sources, and a graph over which breadth, connectivity and bridging can be computed with stated definitions and time windows. DBLP gives computer-science publication records with author names but names are ambiguous; Crossref, OpenAlex and ORCID give identifiers and metadata that can disambiguate some authors but disagree with DBLP and with each other. An answer to "who are the broadest collaborators, how are they connected, and who bridges communities" is useful only if each number can be traced to source records and each identity decision to a policy with a measured error rate. ScholarChain builds that chain: raw records, distributed normalization, an evaluated entity-resolution policy, a temporal coauthorship graph, stores for graph, state and vectors, and a runtime that answers with evidence IDs and refuses or labels claims it cannot support. A second motivation is the course's own: the system had to be generated through a controlled agentic workflow with ownership, evidence, validation, repair and human approval made explicit (course specification, sections "Project goal" and "Agent-generated deliverable evidence"). Sources: [README.md](../README.md) section 2; [modules/rag/RUNTIME-AGENTS.md](../modules/rag/RUNTIME-AGENTS.md).

## 2. The three collaboration research questions and required metrics

The central question and its three decompositions come from the course specification (sections "Required final capability" and "What must be measured"): (1) which researchers have the broadest collaboration networks and the largest author teams; (2) how connected researchers are through coauthors (direct collaborators, shortest paths, components, estimated average path length); (3) which researchers connect otherwise separate communities (communities plus at least one bridge measure, and coreness).

The per-researcher quantities the system computes and keeps distinct are `P_r` (publications), `C_r` (distinct coauthors), `T_r` (total coauthor occurrences), `A_r` (average coauthors per paper), `M_r` (maximum team size, with the responsible works) and `W_r` (weighted degree); per edge: `jointPublicationCount`, `firstCollaborationYear`, `lastCollaborationYear`, `representativeWorkIds` plus the full edge-to-work table; for connectivity: shortest paths with supporting works per hop, component IDs and sizes, a sampled average path length; for bridging: label-propagation communities, cross-community degree, participation coefficient, number of communities touched, and k-core coreness; all of them per time window (2000 to 2004, 2005 to 2009, 2010 to 2014, 2015 to 2019, 2020 to 2024 and an all-time window). Sources: [modules/spark/SPARK-CONTRACTS.md](../modules/spark/SPARK-CONTRACTS.md) sections 4 to 6; [phase-06 agent-report](../docs/agent-runs/phase-06-spark-analytics/agent-report.md).

Two measures were deliberately **not** computed: approximate betweenness centrality (the report states "Approximate betweenness: NOT computed") and, because affiliations are not persisted upstream, institution-based connection analysis ([phase-06 agent-report](../docs/agent-runs/phase-06-spark-analytics/agent-report.md), [phase-07 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-07-storage/human-gate.md) decision 3).

## 4. Agentic software factory architecture

The factory consists of: a constitution ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md), [docs/agents.md](../docs/agents.md)); 18 subagent definitions in [.claude/agents/](../.claude/agents/); five human-started commands in [.claude/commands/](../.claude/commands/) (`/deploy`, `/rollback`, `/incident`, `/run-pipeline`, `/experiment`); hooks and permissions wired in [.claude/settings.json](../.claude/settings.json) (the Phase 01 probe recorded "6 hooks on 4 events"); and a manual validator, [validate-agent-files.sh](../.claude/hooks/validate-agent-files.sh) (a fresh replay output is `[[PENDING-EVAL: replay output not yet committed]]`). Four MCP servers are configured by variable name only ([.mcp.json](../.mcp.json)), and were not enabled in Phase 01 ([phase-01 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-01-agent-factory/human-gate.md)). A phase runs as: the human opens a fresh orchestrator session and supplies the work order; the orchestrator dispatches the owning subagent; the owner inspects state, does only its owned work, runs deterministic verification and returns the 14-field report; the orchestrator dispatches test-engineer and code-reviewer where required; findings are routed to the owner, never fixed by the reviewer; the phase is rerun until its gate passes; the human ratifies, commits and records the hash ([docs/agents.md](../docs/agents.md) sections 2 and 3; course specification, section "How to execute the agentic build"). Subagents never invoke one another; code-reviewer has no write or shell tools. The definition of done is `sbt check` and the marker file `.claude/.last-test-run` written by the canonical command.

Why this design: the specification requires disjoint ownership, hooks and human gates; the project also needs an observable record. The evidence is the per-phase folder under [docs/agent-runs/](../docs/agent-runs/) (prompt, agent report, review report, test report, repairs, human gate, commit hash). Evidence class: the factory is exercised by Phase 01 probes (class F: 7 of 7 PASS) and by every later phase; it is not a measurement of agent quality, which section 19 addresses.

## 8. Entity-resolution method and evaluation

The policy `er-v1` combines exact identifiers (DOI, ORCID, arXiv) with blocking keys and a weighted feature set `er-features-v2` (name similarity, venue and affiliation features that score a neutral 0.5 on real data because those fields are not persisted, and so on), with separate accept and reject thresholds for researchers and works, a CONFLICT outcome that an LLM adjudicator can never override, and an optional LLM adjudication band with a strict output schema, abstention, fabricated-ID rejection, bounded retries and a call cap ([phase-05 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-05-entity-resolution/human-gate.md) section 2; [modules/entity-resolution/ER-CONTRACTS.md](../modules/entity-resolution/ER-CONTRACTS.md)). Policy decisions made by the human include: a plain ORCID disagreement gives REJECTED, CONFLICT only beside strong match evidence; an ORCID match is accepted only if the names fit; Unicode dashes are normalized before tokenizing; accepted pairs are not unioned into clusters until a cannot-link rule exists (implemented in Phase 06).

Gold set and split (class R). The human labeled all 240 queue rows in one session of about 25 minutes through a local display tool that shows only the blind queue columns and writes only the label and notes columns; no agent created, edited or suggested a label; a script confirmed 0 changed non-label cells ([phase-05 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-05-entity-resolution/human-gate.md) section 5). ## 10. Storage architecture

Each store has a different job. Ordinary S3 holds raw, curated, graph, rag, report and artifact objects in five buckets with different lifecycle and evidence rules (raw and reports are versioned and never expire; intermediates expire after 7 to 30 days). Neptune Serverless holds the graph for bounded traversal: loaded by an openCypher bulk-load CSV, queried only through six allowlisted parameterized templates with enforced depth, result and timeout bounds. DynamoDB holds operational state with conditional writes (RunLedger, ArtifactRegistry, EntityLinkCache, QuerySession, EvidenceBundle) and ten analytics lookup tables; on-demand capacity, TTL only on session and cache tables, deletion protection on by default. S3 Vectors holds embeddings (next section); OpenSearch was **not implemented** (the optional tier) ([STORAGE-CONTRACTS.md](../modules/storage/STORAGE-CONTRACTS.md); [INFRA-CONTRACTS.md](../infra/terraform/INFRA-CONTRACTS.md) section 4).

Design decisions recorded: ports in `core` returning `Either[StorageError, A]` keep adapters swappable and AWS-free for tests; Neptune array and map properties are stored as JSON strings because the documentation advises against array properties; provenance digests on rows keep items small with the full provenance once per load manifest; the S3 key-segment limit was tightened to 240 bytes ([phase-07 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-07-storage/human-gate.md) decisions 1, 2 and 4).

## 11. S3 Vectors and optional OpenSearch design

S3 Vectors is used because the vector workload is small (instructor-approved up to 10,000 vectors), needs no always-on cluster and supports metadata filters. Five production indexes (`researcher-profile-v1`, `collaboration-community-v1`, `paper-abstract-v1`, `collaboration-path-v1`, `topic-institution-summary-v1`) plus a dedicated test index; float32, cosine, dimension 1024 (Titan Text Embeddings V2 default; an index dimension cannot change). Vector metadata is compact and never holds long text: it holds `textHash`, `textS3Uri`, `embeddingModelId`, `embeddingModelVersion` and `runId`, so every hit points back to its source text. Writes use batches of at most 500 vectors and 20 MiB; there is no conditional put, so the loader reads existing vectors first, an identical reload is an idempotent no-op, and a different text hash under the same key is rejected; per-key claims in a DynamoDB table (conditional put) guard concurrent workers. The worker embeds, writes, deletes the message on success, retries and dead-letters poison messages after bounded attempts ([STORAGE-CONTRACTS.md](../modules/storage/STORAGE-CONTRACTS.md) sections 5 and 12; limits in [docs/service-limits.md](../docs/service-limits.md)).

Local result (class F, deterministic local embedder, no Bedrock): 9,958 documents rendered across the five indexes and 9,958 vectors written; the second pass with a new run ID skipped all 9,958 with 0 embedding calls; 0 dead-lettered; smoke 25 of 25 (self-retrieval only); two local runs byte-identical (19,921 files) ([phase-08 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-08-embeddings-vectors/human-gate.md)). Paper vectors at 1K hold title-level text only because no abstracts exist upstream at that scale; the 50K OpenAlex fetch includes abstracts. **The approved real-vector target (up to 10,000 vectors, actual count to be reported) is not met by any AWS run yet:** `[[PENDING-AWS: actual vector count, ingestion throughput and retrieval, Phase 15c experiment C]]`. ## 12. Runtime collaboration-analysis agents

The runtime workflow is a pipeline of deterministic stages with a model used for planning and wording only ([modules/rag/RUNTIME-AGENTS.md](../modules/rag/RUNTIME-AGENTS.md)): QueryPlanner (the model proposes an allowlisted plan, code validates it, with a deterministic fallback); ResearcherResolver (candidates from vector hits, abstaining on ambiguous names); CollaborationMetrics (exact stored values with run and metric version, never taken from vector similarity); PathFinder (bounded parameterized graph templates, every hop with supporting works); CommunityAnalyst; SemanticContext (vector results are context only); EvidenceBuilder (graph facts kept separate from semantic context); AnswerSynthesis (typed claims with evidence IDs); ClaimVerification (deterministic checks first, an optional model check can only downgrade); Audit (conditional write to the curated bucket under `rag/audit/`). Controls: retrieved text is data in labeled untrusted segments; every model output is validated against a versioned JSON schema with at most two malformed retries; prompts and schemas are versioned files under [prompts/](../prompts/) whose SHA-256 is recorded per query; no chain of thought is requested or stored. The Bedrock-only runtime decision (Haiku 4.5 through the `us.` inference profile, Titan Embed v2) is in [README.md](../README.md) section 13, including four mismatches between committed defaults and the decision.

Results (class F, fake model client, stores fed from the Phase 07 and 08 local outputs): a 44-question benchmark with expected values computed by a deterministic generator from the Phase 07 tables passes 44 of 44 in two composed runs, byte-identical; 4 of 4 ambiguous-name questions abstain with at least 2 candidates; 4 of 4 prompt-injection questions pass ([phase-09 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-09-runtime-agents/human-gate.md)). The Phase 09 review found three HIGH defects in the claim verifier (digits inside IDs counting as covered, a claim with references but no assertion verifying, assertions not bound to field, subject or window) and two more in the reread (string and boolean assertions not bound to the text, wrong-subject binding); they were repaired and reread. The remaining wording gap (a joint publication count presented as one researcher's total, N5) and N6, N7 were fixed in Phase 10 ([phase-10 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-10-services/human-gate.md) section 2). The optional LLM verification is off by default, so a phrasing outside the verifier's closed lists can still reach an answer as supported; the narrowed homonym rule can miss a same-name researcher ranked below the top 100 vector hits. The live model path (Bedrock Converse) was compiled and tested with fakes only: `[[PENDING-AWS: first live answer, claim support rate, abstention rate, model call counts, tokens and latency]]`.

## 13. Microservice architecture and API

Three independently deployable services run on ECS Fargate: query-api (all public `/v1` routes), retrieval (internal, versioned graph, vector and embed routes; reachable only from query-api) and embedding-worker (an SQS consumer with no listener). They compose `rag` and `storage` at runtime and reach Neptune and S3 Vectors only through retrieval for reads. The REST contract follows the specification: `POST /v1/query` (synchronous within a deadline, otherwise 202 with a job), job status and result, evidence, graph node and bounded neighbors, explain, `/healthz` and `/readyz`; the error model has trace IDs, `Idempotency-Key` handling (same body returns the stored result, a different body is 409), bounded concurrency (4 running, 16 queued, then 429), downstream timeouts, bounded retries and circuit breakers, constant-time API-key authentication with a separate internal key, and structured logs without secrets. The route table is in [docs/api-reference.md](../docs/api-reference.md); contract details in [modules/services/API.md](../modules/services/API.md). Stricter-than-spec choices: any final downstream failure fails the query with 502, 503 or 504 instead of returning a partial answer (accepted by the human, [phase-10 gate](../docs/agent-runs/phase-10-services/human-gate.md) section 2); options the deployment does not honor are refused with 422, so graph-only and vector-only retrieval are not offered ([API.md](../modules/services/API.md)).

Results (classes L and F): 2,555 tests passed at Phase 10; a scripted local session run twice gave identical response pairs; an end-to-end API test reaches an answer whose cited evidence IDs all resolve. ## 24. Lessons learned

The author's own reflection is `[[HUMAN: lessons in the author's words]]`. The following lessons are directly supported by recorded events and may be used as a starting point.

- Independent review caught real defects that the agents' own tests and reports did not: the Phase 03 unapplied fixes, the Phase 04 compressed-input defect, the Phase 09 claim-verifier gaps and the Phase 10 audit-path bug (section 21). The cost was one to five extra rounds per phase.
- Deterministic gates are useful but reveal their limits: the stop hook forced `sbt check` in Phase 02 and blocked 9 times in Phase 15b where only Python had changed; the guard blocks text it cannot interpret, including harmless documentation commands.
- Freezing contracts early (IDs, wire shapes, DOI rule) made later phases additive, and the cost of the one canonical DOI rule being redesigned in Phase 03 was paid once.
- Keeping evaluation labels frozen after seeing held-out results costs accuracy but protects validity (Phase 05).
- Cost control by default-off infrastructure (Neptune, ALB, desired count 0) and human-enforced operating rules is cheap but depends on discipline, as the estimate's sensitivity analysis shows.
- Parallel lanes sharing one machine produce flaky test results; the disclosed fix was running one test task at a time (15 to 38 minute CHECK).
